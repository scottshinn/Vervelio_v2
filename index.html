Know what to build before you build it.

Most product ideas start as a hunch. NidaMind starts with evidence. It listens to what people are already complaining about in public, finds the complaints that keep repeating, and hands you the ones worth building — each with the problem spelled out, who has it, who is already trying to solve it, and where the gap is.

  • Ideas come from real conversations, not guesswork

  • Refreshes itself around the clock, so nothing on the list is stale

[STAGE_03] · BUILD

Weft

Your work, woven into one map.

Your real work is scattered and mostly invisible — half-finished branches on your laptop, decisions buried in a chat that already ended, notes in five places, and nothing tying a broken deploy back to the change that caused it. Weft watches quietly in the background and weaves all of it into one map you can search, share, and pick back up tomorrow.

  • Shows the work actually in progress, not just what has been finished and filed
  • Keeps a readable record of what an AI assistant did and why, long after the chat is gone
  • Notes, tasks and code all linked together and searchable
  • Tells you what has gone quiet, what is overdue, and what just broke
  • Runs on your own machine — your code never has to leave it

Twelve screens, one room.

DoorYard

The most under-used asset in home services is a route that is already moving.

A crew already parked on your street can mow one more lawn for almost nothing extra. DoorYard lets them offer that spare slot, at a set price, to every household nearby who asked to hear about it — and neighbours book it with one tap. The crew fills a gap in the day. The street gets same-day work. Nobody pays for a phone number that never answers.

  • Offers only go out when a crew is genuinely close by
  • You pay when a job is actually booked — never for a lead that goes nowhere
  • Two extra jobs a day is around $1,600 a month of work that would have been missed
  • One tap to book, on the phone people already have
